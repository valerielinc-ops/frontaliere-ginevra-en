Company description

With over 13,000 employees representing 160 trades, the University Hospitals in Geneva are a reference institution at the national and international levels. To learn more about our institution, take a few minutes to consult our 2025 retrospective by clicking here . The position is attached to the Department of Community Medicine and First Recourse (DMCPR). This department combines a societal mission whose aim is to allow access to care for all and an academic mission whose aim is to make pre- and post-graduate education and research in first resort medicine. The first-recourse medical service provides outpatient care for adult patients in primary care. This service also provides outpatient emergency medical services to the population. As part of the HUG's general interest missions, it provides access to care for people who are disadvantaged by their age, their social, economic, legal status (asylum applicants, victims of non-entry, clandestine, prisoners), or their lifestyles

  • If necessary, the first resort medical service also ensures the continuity of their care.

The consultation for victims of torture and war (CTG) is an outpatient service that offers adults traumatized by extreme violence medical, psychological and psycho-social support. It is part of the national group "Support for torture victims" and is financially supported by the Swiss Red Cross and the HUGs. The Geneva GTC has existed since 2004. She works actively within the group to provide care for these vulnerable people. For survivors of torture, war and persecution to become integrated into our society, it is essential that they include health, autonomy and dignity. The multidisciplinary offer of our outpatient service allows them to better manage their traumatic experiences, develop their personal skills and strengthen their family and social resources.

Description of post

You welcome, evaluate and treat people over the age of 16 suffering from the sequelae of torture or war (physical, psychological, social), or their relatives. Professionals and professionals from international and humanitarian organizations can also be cared for and cared for. You evaluate the types of torture, their impact on mental, physical and social health with different tools. You make observations and reports. You also assess other types of interpersonal violence experienced and acted upon. You treat patients using an interdisciplinary approach (close collaboration with other colleagues in the unit and service, and the Geneva network for migrants). Possibly, you will take in support of other types of victims (cases of domestic violence, trafficking in human beings, forced marriages...). You take part in the activities of the first resort medical service (medical college, training, working groups, ongoing research projects, ...). You are involved in the CTG's external activities (external training, representation, etc.) and internal activities of the CTG (team collaborations, supervisions, intervisions, development and application of specific assessment tools, etc.) as well as in network activities at cantonal and federal level. You will be asked for specific involvement in the deployment of the GTC activity (strategy development, participation in the visibility of the consultation and needs assessment).

Qualifications

Your course is advanced in psychiatry-psychotherapy, with experience as a head or head of clinic of at least 2 years, with at least 1 year experience in psychiatry with migrants and migrants. You have knowledge of psychotraumatology. You are in receipt of a Federal Medical Diploma or equivalent recognized by the Federal Medical Professions Commission (MEBEKO). You have a cantonal right of practice if you have an FMH title. You have perfect command of the French language (level C1). A good knowledge of English is desired. A good knowledge and personal interest in the problems of migrants, the Swiss health system, the Swiss asylum policy and the field of psychotrauma (especially complex) are required. Working capacity in a multidisciplinary and university hospital context to ensure comprehensive patient care (contact with nurses, social workers, lawyers and lawyers, interpreters, the care network such as the Asylum Health Unit, MEME, the psychiatric network and associations). You have a great ability to listen and know how to create links. You also manage stress situations. You have the ability to prepare documents and reports for different institutions, in collaboration with consulting physicians. Your interest in teaching and coaching trainees is an asset.
