Who is Sonar?

Sonar enables organizations to ship agentic code with confidence. Code creation is abundant, but confidence is scarce. Our core platform, SonarQube, verifies over 750 billion lines of code every day, delivering agentic verification and governance for all code. Trusted by more than 7 million developers and 75% of the Fortune 100, including teams at Nvidia, ServiceNow, Booking.com , Goldman Sachs, AstraZeneca, and Ford Motor Company, Sonar enables organizations to reduce outages, improve security, and lower the cost and risk of agentic software development.

Founded in Geneva and now headquartered in Austin, Texas, Sonar has a global team working across offices and hubs, including Silicon Valley, London, Bochum, Barcelona, Singapore, Tokyo, and the Washington, D.C. area. At Sonar, we’re enabling enterprises to build agentic software factories that can move quickly while maintaining the control, consistency and evidence required to ship with confidence.

Position description

You will own the product vision and domain strategy for analysis experience, partnering with engineering and product leaders to align investment and delivery across teams.

Your mission is to evolve existing report processing, storage, and APIs into a shared service, so teams can submit work, process findings, and retrieve reusable artifacts and evidence without rebuilding these capabilities independently. Shape the service around static and agentic workflows, with support for additional analysis types guided by validated customer needs.

The domain covers issue-processing pipelines, durable storage, and supporting APIs and events. You will agree the scope and ownership of shared execution orchestration and other expanded capabilities with engine, infrastructure, and consuming-product teams. Engine teams retain detection logic; consuming teams retain their product workflows and acceptance criteria; workflow and Quality Gate owners retain publication, policy, and merge decisions.

Your direct customers are the technical teams building analysis engines, agent services, guidance capabilities, and other products on this service. Success is the value these teams deliver to developers and enterprises, not the number of shared capabilities shipped.

What you will do

  • Set domain strategy and investment priorities. Shape a multi-year plan across customer value, dependencies, technical debt, adoption, and cost. Align senior leaders on trade-offs and recommend where to increase, redirect, or stop investment as evidence changes.
  • Lead discovery with analysis-engine, agent-service, and consuming-product teams, and customers integrating through APIs. Use customer evidence and market and ecosystem signals to identify shared needs and opportunities for differentiation.
  • Coordinate validation across ambiguous, interdependent initiatives. Prioritize the most consequential unknowns and use prototypes, pilots, and clear outcome measures to make timely continue, pivot, or stop decisions.
  • Shape shared execution capabilities with engine and infrastructure teams, including input contracts, scheduling, dependencies, progress tracking, cancellation, retries, and resource budgets. Support different execution environments rather than require every engine to run in one place.
  • Lead product discovery for issue-processing pipelines, including ingestion, normalization, correlation, deduplication, and tracking findings across code changes. Agree contracts for issue identity and state changes with engine and workflow owners, keeping processing separate from publication and policy decisions.
  • Define how source context, findings, execution traces, proposed fixes, and verification results are stored, connected, discovered, and reused. Make freshness, provenance, coverage, and verification limits explicit so missing or incomplete checks are never treated as passes.
  • Guide investments in shared APIs, events, extensibility, documentation, and integration paths. Reduce engine and consumer onboarding effort and enable independent delivery through stable, versioned contracts.
  • Partner with engineering, infrastructure, and security on reliability, scalability, tenant isolation, access control, retention, and observability. Balance latency, coverage, compute and inference cost, and operational complexity.
  • Sequence migrations and deprecations with engineering leaders while preserving existing workflows. Prove value through complete customer workflows before generalizing, and resolve cross-team dependencies without compromising reliability or compatibility.
  • Shape launch, onboarding, and enablement for significant domain releases. Align consuming teams and customer-facing partners on documentation, operational support, and relevant pricing or packaging implications. Follow releases through to adoption and remove systemic barriers to value.
  • Guide quality and launch criteria for shared agentic processing capabilities with engineering, AI specialists, and security. Align leaders on evaluation evidence, fallback behavior, human controls, and cost and latency limits, while consuming teams retain their product-specific acceptance criteria. Lead product decisions from incident learning and establish responsible AI-enabled working practices across the domain.
  • Build domain-wide measurement and experimentation practices. Track integration effort, time to useful results, evidence reuse, reliability, and cost per workflow, connecting these measures to customer and business outcomes.

Experience and qualifications

  • A sustained track record of shaping strategy and delivering measurable outcomes across multiple related technical product areas, particularly developer platforms, APIs, distributed services, workflow orchestration, or data-processing systems. You have translated long-term direction into investment choices, led major transitions, and grown adoption of shared capabilities across teams.
  • Strong technical foundations, ideally developed through software engineering or a similarly hands-on role. You can read code, explore APIs, interpret traces, and build small prototypes to test assumptions.
  • Fluency in asynchronous and event-driven systems, queues, durable execution, data models, API versioning, and failure handling. You can discuss architectural trade-offs credibly without taking design ownership away from engineering.
  • An understanding of software development workflows, including repositories, branches and commits, builds, CI/CD, testing, and how analysis findings are investigated and resolved.
  • Practical understanding of AI-agent workflows and their operational implications, including tool execution, nondeterministic outcomes, context reuse, verification, and cost controls. You can guide shared quality standards and acceptable-risk decisions with specialists, learn from production failures, and establish responsible AI-enabled product practices without outsourcing judgment.
  • Strong strategic product judgment backed by discovery, market insight, and validation experience. You identify opportunities across related product areas, distinguish reusable customer needs from one-off requests, and challenge unnecessary abstraction. You balance near-term commitments with long-term differentiation and use outcome evidence and opportunity cost to guide investment.
  • Demonstrated ability to align senior leaders on domain strategy and contested investment choices, anticipate systemic risks, and resolve ownership ambiguities across teams. You raise product craft across the domain through mentoring PMs, improving decision and review practices, and removing systemic collaboration barriers.
  • Clear written and verbal communication, with the ability to turn interconnected technical and customer problems into a coherent strategic narrative. You move credibly between detailed engineering discussions and senior-leadership decisions, making uncertainty and trade-offs explicit.
