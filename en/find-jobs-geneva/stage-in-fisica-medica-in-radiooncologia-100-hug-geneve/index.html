Company description

With more than 13,000 employees representing 160 professions, the University Hospitals of Geneva (HUG) is a national and international reference institution. To learn more about our institution, take a few minutes to consult our 2025 retrospective by clicking here . The Department of Oncology provides diagnostic and therapeutic care for adult patients and patients with cancer and for the support of patients and their relatives. The aim is to ensure access to the best anticancer treatment, to offer appropriate care and to anticipate the needs of hospitalized patients (mell transplant, cell therapy, immunotherapy, etc.). Together, the Cancer Centre enables coordination of cross-cutting activities on cancer issues between departments and departments. The mission of the Radio-Oncology Service is to assess the therapeutic indication of the persons referred to it, to provide information about radiotherapy, to develop and apply ionizing radiation treatments and to carry out clinical surveillance of the patient. Radio-oncology is particularly used in the context of tumour diseases.

Radio-surgery, total body irradiation and contact therapy treatments are also performed.

Description of post

You are part of the medical physics group of the HUG Radio Oncology Service, which consists of 7 physicists and 4 dosimetrists. The course is part of a project to optimise quality control procedures for external radiotherapy equipment, quality control of treatment plans and doses to patients, and to automate certain tasks in the patient file path.

Qualifications

You are in the course of the Master's degree (preferably 2nd year) and you are looking for a compulsory and agreed internship in the field of medical physics to validate your course. You are registered or registered with your university for the duration of the internship, which must be completed in full before the date of graduation. You have knowledge of medical physics and are autonomous with a good ability to adapt to a multidisciplinary team (physicists and physicists, technicians and technicians, doctors, dosimetrists, nurses, etc.). Your knowledge of the Swiss hospital community is an asset. You have an excellent relationship. You have or have a very good level of oral and written French, as well as a good level of English.
